Abstract Heatwaves are becoming more frequent, intense and prolonged as the climate warms, placing stress on the electricity infrastructure underpinning modern societies. Yet how rising temperatures erode grid capacity remains under-quantified. Existing power-flow models either oversimplify thermal effects or become computationally intractable when resolving them in detail. Here we introduce a unified, computationally efficient framework that jointly captures temperature-dependent demand, generator derating and spatially resolved transmission-line thermal limits within an alternating-current optimal power-flow formulation. Applying it to the Western European system with open-access grid, high-resolution weather reanalysis and demand data, we simulate hundreds of projected heatwave scenarios for 2026–2030. Across these represented grid configurations, the analysis reveals substantial national disparities: the Spanish grid exhibits temperature-induced bottlenecks and load shedding, whereas the German grid remains comparatively resilient. Heatwave-aware power-flow analysis thus uncovers structural bottlenecks and should be integrated into grid planning and adaptation as heatwaves intensify under climate change.
